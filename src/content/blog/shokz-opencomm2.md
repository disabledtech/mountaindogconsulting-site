The Shokz OpenComm2 is the first headset that's let me hold a conversation in a busy mall without raising my voice. I'd recommend it more widely if it got along better with my headrest. (I bought the standard model myself for $174.95 CAD and have no relationship with Shokz.)

My voice is weaker than it used to be. Sitting in a power chair puts my mouth well below the ear of whoever I'm out with, and the chair keeps us a foot or two further apart than two people walking side by side. Add the acoustics of a mall (high ceilings, hard floors, background music, a few hundred other conversations) and I spent a lot of outings repeating myself and missing half of what was said back.

These days, when I head out with someone, we start a FaceTime audio call before we leave. I wear the OpenComm2, and they wear whatever headset they prefer, usually AirPods. It sounds a little silly written down (yes, I'm on a phone call with someone a metre away), but it works. FaceTime does need an Apple device on at least one end. Someone on Android can join from a browser with a FaceTime link, and WhatsApp or another calling app should do the same job, though I haven't tried them.

I chose FaceTime because I'd been told it sounds better than a regular call, and that's partly true. FaceTime can carry far more of the human voice than a cellular call, but most Bluetooth headsets, this one included, squeeze the sound back down before it ever reaches the call.

The boom (the short arm that holds the microphone) puts it a couple of centimetres from the corner of my mouth, with noise reduction behind it, and I can speak far more quietly than I ever would to be heard in public and still come through clearly on the other end. I've tested it in several malls since buying it this summer, around crowds and music, and it has worked (*almost*) every time.

At home I wear AirPods Max, which I love, but they're big and they cut me off from the room. That's fine when I'm working at my desk, but not at a checkout counter. The OpenComm2 uses bone conduction (small pads rest on your cheekbones and carry sound through the bone, leaving your ears open), so I still hear the cashier, the person beside me, and whatever is happening around my chair. It also draws far less attention than a pair of over-ear headphones.

In a vehicle, Siri and Apple's Voice Control have never been able to pick me out from the road noise, with or without the AirPods Max. Your experience will depend on how loud your vehicle is, but in mine the built-in microphones hear the engine and the road far better than they hear me. With the OpenComm2, both work well enough to use without me raising my voice, though they still miss now and then. I hadn't bought it for that, so it was a nice surprise.

It charges over USB-C, which suits me, since I've been aggressively pruning older USB connectors from my life. It paired with my iPhone on the first try, and with my Mac the one time I tried that. I haven't timed the battery, but I've never had a moment of battery anxiety on a full day out. I wear glasses, and I find it more comfortable to put the headset on first and the glasses second. Your mileage may vary.

I can't put any headset on or take it off by myself, or press its buttons, so I don't count either against this one (and I can't tell you much about the buttons). My complaint is the band. It's a single fixed size, and even on my larger head it sits 2 to 3 cm out from the back of my skull, so anything behind me hits the band first. When I'm driving my chair I'm usually not leaning back, so it's fine. In a vehicle, or otherwise at rest (sitting at a table eating, say), my head goes back against the headrest, the band presses into it, and the ear hooks pull down on my ears. Thirty minutes to an hour is tolerable, but the pressure is constant and it only gets worse. If you can't lift your head off the headrest for some relief, I'd expect it to become a real problem, because ears are one of the most common places for medical devices, like oxygen tubing, to cause pressure injuries.

Shokz sells the OpenComm2 as an office headset for people who spend the day on calls. I'd also suggest it to anyone whose voice doesn't carry in a noisy room and who still wants to hear what's around them. Keep in mind that it's a one-to-one fix, since it only works because there's a call with one person on the other end. It won't help you be heard by a group, or by the cashier. A portable voice amplifier will, and I've used them, but they have their own drawbacks. In a store I don't want my half of the conversation broadcast to everyone nearby, and an amplifier sends my voice wherever it happens to be pointed. Walking side by side, that's roughly toward my companion. Turned around in an aisle, it's anyone's guess. For a conversation with one person, I'd take the headset.

If your head rests against a headrest for most of the day, I'd pass on this one, because the band catches on it every time you lean back. If it doesn't, and you've been repeating yourself in malls the way I was, it's worth a try.
